# AI Product Building for Kids
## A 5-Week Syllabus for Elementary Students

**Teacher**: Henry
**Background**: Video/Coding + Engineering
**Goal**: Teach kids how products are built with AI — from idea to launch

---

## Course Overview

| Week | Theme | Big Question |
|------|-------|-------------|
| 1 | User Experience | "Who is this for and what do they need?" |
| 2 | Talking to AI | "How do I give good instructions?" |
| 3 | Build What Matters First | "What's the ONE thing that must work?" |
| 4 | Will It Work for Everyone? | "What happens when 100 people use it?" |
| 5 | Think Like an Engineer | "How do I pick the best way to build it?" |

---

## Week 1: User Experience

> **Big Idea**: Before you build anything, understand WHO you're building for and WHAT problem you're solving.

### Day 1 — Who Is This For?

**Topic**: Every product is made for a specific person.

**Explain (5 min)**:
- A toy store for babies looks different than one for 10-year-olds
- You can't build something for "everyone" — pick someone specific

**Activity (15 min)**:
- Draw your "user" on paper
- Write 3 things they like and 2 things that are hard for them

**Takeaway**: "I need to know my user before I build anything."

---

### Day 2 — What's Their Problem?

**Topic**: A good product fixes one specific annoying thing.

**Explain (5 min)**:
- Umbrellas solve: getting wet in rain
- Backpacks solve: carrying too many things in your hands
- What problem does YOUR idea solve?

**Activity (15 min)**:
- Interview a classmate: "What's one thing that bugs you at school?"
- Write down their answer as: "[Name] is frustrated because ___"

**Takeaway**: "A good product starts with a real problem."

---

### Day 3 — Show, Don't Confuse

**Topic**: If someone can't figure it out in 5 seconds, it's too complicated.

**Explain (5 min)**:
- Show two TV remotes: one with 5 buttons, one with 50
- Which one would a kid pick up and use right away?

**Activity (15 min)**:
- Look at 2 app screens (printed or on board)
- Circle which is easier to understand
- Write one reason WHY

**Takeaway**: "Simple is better. Don't make people think too hard."

---

### Day 4 — Let Them Try It

**Topic**: You don't know if it works until someone else uses it.

**Explain (5 min)**:
- You know how YOUR drawing works because you made it
- But does your FRIEND know? That's the real test.

**Activity (15 min)**:
- Swap your Day 1 drawing with a partner
- Partner tries to "use" it without any help from you
- Partner writes: "I understood ___" and "I was confused by ___"

**Takeaway**: "Testing with real people shows you what to fix."

---

### Day 5 — Fix What Confused Them

**Topic**: Feedback is a gift. Use it to make things better.

**Explain (5 min)**:
- Getting feedback doesn't mean you failed
- It means you now know exactly what to improve
- Pro builders do this over and over

**Activity (15 min)**:
- Read your partner's feedback from yesterday
- Redraw your screen fixing the confusing parts
- Show both versions: before and after

**Takeaway**: "Version 2 is always better because of feedback."

---

## Week 2: Talking to AI

> **Big Idea**: AI is a fast helper, but it only works well when YOU give clear, specific instructions.

### Day 6 — Be Specific

**Topic**: "Make something cool" won't work. "Make a red house with a blue door" will.

**Explain (5 min)**:
- Imagine telling a robot to "make food" — what would happen?
- Now tell it: "Make a peanut butter sandwich on white bread, cut in triangles"
- More details = better result

**Activity (15 min)**:
- "Robot Game": One partner is the "robot" with eyes closed
- Other partner gives step-by-step instructions to draw a house
- Compare the instruction vs the result

**Takeaway**: "The more specific I am, the better AI can help me."

---

### Day 7 — Show an Example

**Topic**: AI works better when you show what you want, not just tell.

**Explain (5 min)**:
- If you say "write a poem" you could get anything
- If you say "write a poem LIKE THIS ONE" and show an example, it matches your style

**Activity (15 min)**:
- Give a partner (pretending to be AI) a vague request. See what they make.
- Now give them the SAME request + an example. See the difference.
- Discuss: which result was closer to what you wanted?

**Takeaway**: "Showing an example helps AI understand what I mean."

---

### Day 8 — Check the Answer

**Topic**: AI is fast but not always right. You must double-check.

**Explain (5 min)**:
- Spell-check says "their" but you meant "there" — it doesn't know!
- AI can write something that SOUNDS right but IS wrong
- Your job: be the checker

**Activity (15 min)**:
- Teacher gives a "story written by AI" with 3 hidden mistakes (wrong facts, silly errors)
- Kids find and circle all the mistakes
- Discuss: "What would have happened if no one checked?"

**Takeaway**: "Always check AI's work. I'm smarter about MY project than AI is."

---

### Day 9 — One Step at a Time

**Topic**: Big requests confuse AI. Small requests get good answers.

**Explain (5 min)**:
- If you ask "plan my whole birthday party" — that's too much at once
- Better: "First, help me pick a theme. Now help me make an invite."
- Small steps = better results each time

**Activity (15 min)**:
- Pick a big task: "Plan a class field trip"
- Break it into 5 small steps on sticky notes
- Put them in order on the board

**Takeaway**: "Break big problems into small steps."

---

### Day 10 — Try Again Differently

**Topic**: If the first answer isn't good, don't give up — ask a different way.

**Explain (5 min)**:
- Same question, different words can get a totally different answer
- It's like asking for directions: "How do I get to the park?" vs "What's the fastest way to the park from school?"

**Activity (15 min)**:
- Everyone gets the same question
- Write it 3 different ways
- Compare: which version got the best answer?
- Vote on the best way to ask

**Takeaway**: "If AI gives a bad answer, try asking differently."

---

## Week 3: Build What Matters First (P0 vs P1)

> **Big Idea**: Build the thing that MUST work first. Extras come later.

### Day 11 — Must-Have vs Nice-to-Have

**Topic**: Some things are required. Some things are bonus.

**Explain (5 min)**:
- A bike MUST have: wheels, handlebars, seat, pedals
- A bike BONUS: bell, basket, stickers, light
- Without wheels it's not a bike. Without stickers it's still a bike.

**Activity (15 min)**:
- Card sort game: teacher hands out feature cards for a "School Lunch App"
- Kids sort into two piles: MUST HAVE (P0) ✓ and NICE TO HAVE (P1) ★
- Groups compare and defend their choices

**Takeaway**: "P0 = it breaks without this. P1 = it's better with this."

---

### Day 12 — What's the ONE Thing?

**Topic**: If your product only does one thing, what should it be?

**Explain (5 min)**:
- Google's first page: just a search box. That's it. One thing, done perfectly.
- What's YOUR app's one thing?

**Activity (15 min)**:
- Pick a product idea: "Homework Helper App"
- Each kid writes their #1 most important feature on a card
- Stand up and defend: "This should be #1 because ___"
- Class votes

**Takeaway**: "Find the ONE thing that matters most. Build that first."

---

### Day 13 — Smallest Working Version

**Topic**: Get something working FIRST, even if it's basic.

**Explain (5 min)**:
- A paper airplane that flies 5 feet > a fancy one that doesn't fly at all
- Working and simple beats fancy and broken

**Activity (15 min)**:
- Build the SIMPLEST paper airplane that flies (no decorations, basic fold)
- Test: does it fly? Yes = success!
- Rule: you cannot decorate until it flies

**Takeaway**: "Make it work first. Make it pretty later."

---

### Day 14 — Add One Thing at a Time

**Topic**: After the basics work, add ONE improvement. Test. Repeat.

**Explain (5 min)**:
- Your airplane flies. Now pick ONE change: pointier nose? Bigger wings?
- Change one thing, then test again
- If you change 5 things and it breaks, you won't know which one broke it

**Activity (15 min)**:
- Add ONE modification to your airplane
- Test: did it fly better, worse, or the same?
- Write down what you changed and what happened

**Takeaway**: "Change one thing at a time so you know what works."

---

### Day 15 — Saying "Not Yet"

**Topic**: Good builders say "later" to good ideas so they can finish the important stuff.

**Explain (5 min)**:
- If you try to build a bike AND a skateboard AND a scooter at the same time, you finish nothing
- "Not yet" doesn't mean "never" — it means "after the important stuff is done"

**Activity (15 min)**:
- Class brainstorms 20 features for a "Class Pet Tracker" app
- Vote: which 3 go into version 1?
- Rest go on a "LATER" poster on the wall

**Takeaway**: "Saying 'not yet' to good ideas helps me finish the great ones."

---

## Week 4: Will It Work for Everyone? (Scalability)

> **Big Idea**: What works for 5 people might break for 500. Think ahead.

### Day 16 — Works for 1 vs Works for 100

**Topic**: Something that works for you might break when the whole school uses it.

**Explain (5 min)**:
- You can make lemonade for 3 friends with one pitcher
- What if the whole school shows up? One pitcher won't work.
- You need a bigger plan

**Activity (15 min)**:
- Simulation: 1 kid hands out papers to the class (time it)
- Now 3 kids hand out papers (time it)
- Discuss: what changed? Why was it faster?

**Takeaway**: "Always ask: what happens if WAY more people show up?"

---

### Day 17 — The Line Problem

**Topic**: When too many people wait, the system is too slow.

**Explain (5 min)**:
- Lunch with 1 window = long line, everyone is hungry and waiting
- Lunch with 3 windows = short lines, everyone eats faster
- The "line" is called a bottleneck

**Activity (15 min)**:
- Simulate: everyone "orders" from 1 kid (server) — time how long for whole class
- Now split into 3 lines with 3 servers — time again
- Calculate the difference

**Takeaway**: "Find the bottleneck — the one slow spot — and fix it."

---

### Day 18 — Organize So You Find Things

**Topic**: Labeled bins beat one big pile.

**Explain (5 min)**:
- Imagine finding ONE specific LEGO piece in a giant unsorted box
- Now imagine sorted bins: red pieces here, flat pieces there
- Organization = speed

**Activity (15 min)**:
- Race: find a specific item in a messy pile vs a sorted set
- Time both
- Discuss: what made the sorted one faster?

**Takeaway**: "Organized information is fast information."

---

### Day 19 — Rules Keep Things Safe

**Topic**: Good rules prevent things from breaking.

**Explain (5 min)**:
- "Max 4 kids on the trampoline" = safety rule
- Without rules: trampoline breaks, someone gets hurt
- Products need rules too: "max 100 people in this chat room"

**Activity (15 min)**:
- Scenario: class library with no rules (books everywhere, lost, torn)
- Kids write 3 rules to keep the library working
- Share and vote on the best rules

**Takeaway**: "Rules protect the product and the people using it."

---

### Day 20 — Plan for More People

**Topic**: Build the foundation bigger than you need today.

**Explain (5 min)**:
- If you plant a garden for 5 plants but KNOW you'll want 50 later...
- ...leave space now! Don't build a fence right around the 5 plants.

**Activity (15 min)**:
- Draw a school garden for 5 plants
- Now on the SAME paper, show how it could grow to 50 plants
- Did you leave room? Or do you have to start over?

**Takeaway**: "Think about tomorrow's size when building today."

---

## Week 5: Think Like an Engineer (Review & Decide)

> **Big Idea**: Engineers don't just build. They compare options and pick the best approach.

### Day 21 — What It Must Do (Functional Requirements)

**Topic**: First, write down what your product MUST do.

**Explain (5 min)**:
- A backpack MUST: hold books, have straps, close shut
- These are the JOBS it has to do — no exceptions
- If it can't do these, it's not a backpack

**Activity (15 min)**:
- Pick a product (lunchbox, bike helmet, water bottle)
- List 3-5 things it MUST do
- Share: does the class agree? Anything missing?

**Takeaway**: "Write down what it MUST do before you build it."

---

### Day 22 — How Well It Does It (Non-Functional Requirements)

**Topic**: It's not enough to work — it should work WELL.

**Explain (5 min)**:
- Two backpacks both hold books. But one is lighter, waterproof, and comfortable.
- "How well" it works matters: fast, safe, easy, reliable
- These are called non-functional requirements

**Activity (15 min)**:
- Rate 3 backpacks (or pictures) on a scorecard:
  - Light? (1-5)
  - Waterproof? (1-5)
  - Comfortable? (1-5)
  - Looks cool? (1-5)
- Which wins overall? Why?

**Takeaway**: "Good isn't just 'does it work' — it's 'how well does it work.'"

---

### Day 23 — Compare Options

**Topic**: There's usually more than one way to build something. Pick the best.

**Explain (5 min)**:
- 3 ways to cross a river: swim, build a bridge, take a boat
- Each has trade-offs: speed, safety, cost
- Engineers compare before choosing

**Activity (15 min)**:
- Challenge: build a "bridge" from paper/popsicle sticks/cardboard
- 3 groups, 3 different materials
- Test: which holds the most weight (pencils)?
- Discuss trade-offs: which was fastest to build? Strongest? Cheapest?

**Takeaway**: "Compare options before picking. There's always a trade-off."

---

### Day 24 — Check AI's Work Like an Engineer

**Topic**: AI gives you a first draft. Your job is to make it better.

**Explain (5 min)**:
- AI designed a lunchbox but: the lid doesn't close, it's too heavy, no handle
- AI doesn't KNOW these are problems — YOU do because you understand the user
- Engineer's job: find problems, fix them

**Activity (15 min)**:
- Teacher shows "AI's design" for a lunchbox (drawing with 3 intentional problems)
- Kids identify the problems
- Kids sketch a fixed version
- Present: "AI got this wrong because ___. I fixed it by ___."

**Takeaway**: "AI builds fast. I build smart. Together we're unstoppable."

---

### Day 25 — Version 2 Is Always Better (Final Project)

**Topic**: Real engineers improve things after testing. Show your growth.

**Explain (5 min)**:
- Look at your Week 1 drawing. You've learned SO much since then.
- Now you know about: users, testing, P0, scale, trade-offs
- Time to rebuild with everything you know

**Activity (20 min)**:
- Take your original Week 1 product idea
- Redesign it using everything from 5 weeks:
  - Who's it for? (Week 1)
  - What instructions would you give AI to help? (Week 2)
  - What's the P0? (Week 3)
  - How does it handle lots of users? (Week 4)
  - What are the requirements? (Week 5)
- Present BEFORE and AFTER to the class

**Takeaway**: "I'm now a product thinker. I can build things that people actually need."

## Assessment

No tests. Assessment is through:
- **Daily takeaway** (kids write/draw one thing they learned)
- **Participation in activities**
- **Final Day 25 presentation** (before/after comparison)
