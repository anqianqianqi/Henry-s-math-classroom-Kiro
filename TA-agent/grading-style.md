## Philosophy
This teacher values correct solutions, clear logical reasoning, and creativity in solving problems. They appreciate concise and efficient problem-solving techniques. **Brevity is acceptable when the core idea is right** — the teacher does not require verbose explanations on every problem, especially concept-check problems where a short correct statement is sufficient.

## Point Distribution Guidelines
- **Full Marks (3 or 5 points)**: Awarded for accurate and complete solutions. On concept-check problems, correctly stating the right idea — even briefly — earns full marks. For calculation/proof problems, clear logical steps are required.
- **Partial Marks (2 points)**: Given for partially correct or reasonable attempts where the method is right but a case is missed (e.g. b=0), or the approach is correct but an error in execution.
- **Minimum Marks (1 point)**: For minimal effort, assertions without justification (especially in geometry), or significant logical gaps.

## Partial Credit Rules
- Partial credit is given when the student's method is correct but they missed an edge case (e.g. dividing by a variable without checking if it equals zero).
- If the concept is right but there is an arithmetic or algebraic execution error, partial credit is granted.
- Students who attempt multiple approaches, even if some are incomplete, get credit for the correct parts.

## How the Teacher Uses Comments
- Comments guide students toward discovering mistakes rather than directly stating the error — questions like "but what if...?" are common.
- Positive reinforcement is frequent: "Very good!", "沒錯!!", "That's exactly right!", "很棒!!".
- When a student's method is creative but incomplete, the teacher acknowledges the good idea before pointing out what's missing.
- Comments sometimes extend the problem ("next time can you try...") — these are not grade deductions, just encouragement.

## What Leads to Higher Scores
- Correctly identifying the core mathematical insight, even stated briefly.
- Step-by-step verification of all cases (e.g. checking each value of a that makes a factor zero).
- Finding the right answer by a creative or unexpected method — full credit regardless of method.
- Showing algebraic derivation for geometry problems rather than just asserting angle values.

## What Leads to Deductions
- Missing an edge case that changes the answer (e.g. b=0 when dividing by b).
- Asserting a geometry result without any algebraic or logical derivation.
- Providing only a final answer with no explanation on problems where reasoning matters.
- Logical gaps or unsupported assumptions in the solution chain.

## Critical Exceptions (derived from real grading patterns)

**Short answers on concept problems = full credit**
If the problem is asking for understanding/concept (e.g. "which base system represents larger numbers?"), a brief correct answer like "3进制大" gets full marks. Do NOT penalize brevity when the answer is correct.

**The b=0 rule**
When a student divides both sides of an equation by a variable (e.g. divides by b), they MUST address the case where that variable equals zero. Omitting this gets 2/3 instead of 3/3, even if the rest is correct.

**Geometry: assertions without algebra = 1 point**
For angle/geometry proofs, simply stating "angle X > Y degrees because angle Z is less than..." without derivation gets 1 point. Algebraic steps or logical chains using known angle relationships are required for 3+ points.

**Image submissions are valid**
If a student writes "Please check the attached" or similar, their actual answer is in the image. Treat these as potentially correct — do not give 0 just because the text is empty. Grade as "needs human review" or ask if image context is unavailable.

**Multiple valid methods = full credit**
A student who reaches the correct answer by an unexpected but mathematically valid method receives full credit. The "standard" method is not required.

**Language is neutral**
Chinese and English answers are equally acceptable. Mixed-language responses are fine.
