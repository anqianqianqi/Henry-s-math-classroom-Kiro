# Henry's Grading Style Guide

> Auto-generated on 2026-07-23 from 200 graded submissions.
> Updated manually after quality evaluation (88% accuracy → targeting 95%+).

---

## Philosophy
This teacher values correct solutions, clear logical reasoning, and creativity in solving problems. They appreciate concise and efficient problem-solving techniques. **Brevity is acceptable when the core idea is right** — the teacher does not require verbose explanations on every problem, especially concept-check problems where a short correct statement is sufficient.

---

## Full Marks Criteria (all problem types)

Award full marks when the student:
- Found the correct answer(s) — all of them
- Showed they understand the underlying reason — even briefly
- Used any valid method — creativity is rewarded, unexpected approaches that reach the right answer get full marks

**CRITICAL: Brief correct answers earn full marks.**
Do NOT penalize:
- Not using the "standard" method
- Not showing every intermediate step if the result is clearly correct
- Brief answers on concept or computation problems
- Answering in a different style than expected

**Correct final answer with no working shown does NOT lose marks.**
If the answer is right, the grade is full marks. The comment can ask for explanation, but missing working is a teaching note, not a penalty.

---

## Partial Marks Criteria (all problem types)

Award partial marks (roughly 2/3) when:
- Correct method, missed one case — approach is sound but left out a constraint or solution
- Right insight, incomplete execution — correct approach but algebraic slip prevents reaching full answer

---

## Minimal Marks Criteria (all problem types)

Award minimal marks (roughly 1/3) when:
- Student showed some relevant thinking but cannot reach the answer from where they are
- Significant logical error or misunderstanding of the problem type

---

## Zero Marks

Award zero only when:
- The submission shows no relevant mathematical engagement
- The student clearly addressed a completely different problem

---

## CRITICAL: Notation vs. Computed Result — Always Trust the Numbers

When a student writes a formula imprecisely (missing parentheses, informal shorthand) but their computed numerical answer is correct, that is **NOT** an error.

**The rule**: Before marking any step wrong, compute it yourself using the student's values at that point. If your computation matches the student's stated answer, they computed correctly — the notation was just sloppy.

**The test for an error is**: does the student's stated number match what the math gives? NOT: does the written formula look syntactically correct?

**Real example (Chloe, 2026-07-23):**
Student wrote "Y = 9/2 - X + Z, which is 4, so Y = 1/2" — notation looks ambiguous, but Y = 9/2 − 4 = 1/2 ✓. The answer is correct. Grade 3/3, not 1/3.

Never penalize notation when the computed answer is right. Judge the math, not the presentation.

---

## Point Distribution Guidelines

- **Full Marks (3 or 5 points)**: Accurate and complete solutions. Correct answer by any valid method, stated even briefly.
- **Partial Marks (2 points)**: Method is right but one case missed, or correct approach with an execution error.
- **Minimum Marks (1 point)**: Minimal effort, assertions without justification, or significant logical gaps.

---

## Partial Credit Rules

- Partial credit is given when the student's method is correct but they missed an edge case
- If the concept is right but there is an arithmetic/algebraic execution error, partial credit is granted
- Students who attempt multiple approaches get credit for the correct parts

## How Henry Writes Comments (all problem types)

**Step 1: Acknowledge the good idea** — always name what the student did right first. This is pedagogically essential.

Examples:
- *"除以b是一個很棒的想法!!"* (Dividing by b is a great idea!!)
- *"很棒! 那如果我把 1/a 整個當一個未知數呢"* (Great! What if...)
- *"Verygood trick! to add up them."*

**Step 2: Pause with "但是" / "But..."** — signals something is missing without being negative.

**Step 3: Ask a question — do not state the answer** — Henry points toward the insight with a question, not a correction.

**Step 4: Optional — suggest the next step** — for students close to the answer.

Henry's tone is consistently warm and encouraging. Never write a comment that only says what's wrong. Always name the right idea first.

---

## Critical Exceptions

**Short answers on concept problems = full credit**
A brief correct answer like "3进制大" (base-3 is bigger) earns full marks. Do NOT penalize brevity when the answer is correct.

**Image submissions are valid**
If a student writes "Please check the attached" or "如图", treat as potentially correct. Do not give 0. Flag for Henry's review with comment: "Looks good! Can you also write out your steps in text next time?"

**Multiple valid methods = full credit**
A student who reaches the correct answer by any mathematically valid method receives full credit. The "standard" method is not required.

**Language is neutral**
Chinese and English answers are equally acceptable. Mixed-language responses are fine.

**Geometry: assertions without algebra = 1 point**
For angle/geometry proofs, stating results without derivation gets 1 point. Algebraic steps or logical chains are required for 3+ points.
