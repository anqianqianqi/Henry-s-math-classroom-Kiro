# Equation Solving — Henry's Grading Rules

> This file is entirely derived from Henry's actual correction log and observed
> grading behavior. It teaches the TA how Henry reads and responds to equation
> submissions — not invented rules, but distilled patterns.

---

## What Henry values when grading equations

Henry is not checking whether the student followed the "right" method.
He is asking: *does this student understand what's happening in this equation?*

A student who writes `a=1, a=2, a=3` because *"one of them must be zero"* has
demonstrated understanding — even if they didn't write out every algebraic step.
(Correction log Example #2: 3/3 with comment "沒錯主要就是其中一個要是0")

A student who divides by b and gets a=2 has shown a smart approach — but missed
asking "what if b=0?" That gap earns partial marks, not zero.
(Correction log Examples #3, #5: 2/3 each)

---

## Full marks criteria

Award full marks when the student:

- **Found the correct answer(s)** — all of them, not just some
- **Showed that they understand the underlying reason** — even briefly
- **Checked or implicitly addressed any constraints** (e.g., b≠0 for denominator equations)
- Used any valid method — unexpected approaches that reach the right answer
  get full marks. Creativity is rewarded.

**CRITICAL: Brief correct answers earn full marks.**
If a student writes `a=1, a=2, a=3` or `a=b=1` or `a=2/3` and their reasoning
(even if short) is mathematically sound, that is full marks. Do NOT penalize:
- Not using the "standard" method
- Not showing every intermediate step if the result is clearly correct
- Brief answers on concept or computation problems
- Answering in a different style than expected

Examples from Henry:
- "a=b=1" with "79+99=178" → 3/3 (saw the structure, got the right answer)
- "1/a - 3/a = 2-5 → -2/a = -3 → a=2/3" → 3/3 (valid algebra, correct answer)
- "a=1,2,3 因为...有一个等于零" → 3/3 (brief but correct reasoning)

---

## Partial marks criteria

Award partial marks (roughly 2/3) when:

- **Correct method, missed one case** — the student's approach is sound, they
  got most of the answer right, but left out one constraint or solution.
  The classic case: dividing by b without considering b=0.
  *"除以b是一個很棒的想法!! 但是....還有一種可能"*

- **Right insight, incomplete execution** — the student understood what type
  of problem this is and started correctly but made an algebraic slip
  that prevents reaching the full answer.

- **Correct answer, no explanation where one is needed** — if the problem
  requires the student to show their reasoning and they give only a final
  answer. Henry's response: *"Can you give me an explanation?"*
  (Correction log Example #11: 2/3, comment "Can you give me an explanation?")

---

## Minimal marks criteria

Award minimal marks (roughly 1/3) when:

- The student **confused the variable structure** — e.g., treated `1/a` as if it
  were `a`, or misread the equation form entirely. This shows they couldn't
  engage with what the equation was actually asking.
  Example: writing `1/a + 5a = 3 + 2a` when the problem has `1/a + 5 = 3 + 2/a`
  — this means the student thought `5` meant `5a` and `1/a` and `2/a` were different
  kinds of terms. The confusion is at the level of reading the equation, not solving it.
- The student identified the equation type correctly but made a fatal algebraic
  error that prevents reaching any valid solution.
- The student showed only the first correct step with no progression and
  the result is wrong.

---

## Zero marks

Award zero only when:

- The submission shows no relevant mathematical engagement.
- The student clearly addressed a completely different problem.

Note: image-only submissions ("Please check the attached") are **not** zero —
treat them as potentially containing a full correct answer that cannot be
verified from text alone. These should be flagged for Henry's review.

## How Henry comments on equation problems

Henry's comments follow a consistent pattern. The TA's suggested comment should
mirror this structure:

**Step 1: Acknowledge the good idea**
Always start by naming what the student did right. This is not courtesy —
it's pedagogically important. Students need to know which part of their thinking
was sound so they build on it.

Examples from correction log:
- *"除以b是一個很棒的想法!!"* (Dividing by b is a great idea!!)
- *"很棒! 那如果我把 1/a 整個當一個未知數呢"* (Great! What if...)
- *"Verygood trick! to add up them."*

**Step 2: Pause with "但是" / "But..." before the gap**
The pivot word signals that something is missing without being negative.
*"但是....還有一種可能"* (But... there is another possibility)

**Step 3: Ask a question — do not state the answer**
Henry points toward the missing insight with a question, not a correction.
- *"如果b等於零的話呢?"* (What if b equals zero?)
- *"還有一種可能....是不能除以b的"* (Another possibility... is that you can't divide by b)
- *"可以再解釋一下嗎?"* (Can you explain further?)

**Step 4: Optional — suggest the next step or a new angle**
For students close to the answer, Henry often suggests the next move:
- *"那如果我把 1/a 整個當一個未知數呢"* (What if I treated 1/a as a single unknown?)
- *"去嘗試更多不同的數字 譬如 1.4 x 1.4"* (Try more different numbers, like 1.4 × 1.4)

---

## Tone

Henry's tone is consistently warm and encouraging, even when giving partial marks.
The message is always: *your thinking is good, here is one more step to take.*

Never write a comment that only says what's wrong. Always name the right idea first.

Language: Chinese and English are both acceptable. Mixed is fine. Match the language
the student used in their submission.
