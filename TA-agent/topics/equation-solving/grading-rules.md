# Equation Solving — Topic-Specific Grading Rules

> This file contains ONLY rules that are specific to equation problems.
> Universal grading rules (full marks criteria, notation vs numbers, comment style, etc.)
> are in TA-agent/grading-style.md and always apply.

---

## What Henry specifically looks for in equation problems

Henry is asking: *does this student understand what's happening in this equation?*
Not: did they follow the expected method?

---

## The b=0 Rule (equation-specific)

When a student divides both sides of an equation by a variable or expression,
they MUST address whether that variable could equal zero.

- Omitting this check → 2/3, even if the rest is correct
- The gap: dividing by b is valid IF b≠0. The b=0 case may produce a different solution or no solution.

Henry's comment pattern: *"除以b是一個很棒的想法!! 但是....還有一種可能....是不能除以b的"*
(Dividing by b is a great idea!! But... there's another possibility — what if b can't be divided?)

**Examples:** Correction log Examples #3, #5 — both 2/3 for missing b=0.

---

## Variable Structure Confusion (equation-specific)

Award minimal marks (1/3) when:
- Student treated `1/a` as if it were `a`, or misread the equation form entirely
- Example: writing `1/a + 5a = 3 + 2a` when the problem has `1/a + 5 = 3 + 2/a`
  — the student confused `5` with `5a` and didn't recognize `1/a` as a reciprocal term

This is confusion at the level of *reading the equation*, not solving it.

---

## 5-Point Problems with Elegant Substitution (equation-specific)

For problems like `a(b+1) + b + 1 = 5` with max 5 points:
- A student who correctly tries b=1 and immediately solves → 5/5
- The insight of finding the simplifying substitution earns full marks regardless of brevity

Examples Henry gave 5/5:
- `b=1 → (5-1)÷1 = 4 → a=4` (two lines) → 5/5
- `We can find: ax(b+1)+b+1=5, b=1 → 2a+2=5 → a=3/2` → 5/5

Do NOT give 1 or 2 out of 5 for "not enough working shown" when the result is clearly correct.

---

## Numerical Approximation Problems (equation-specific)

For problems asking to approximate a value (e.g., "find a where a² ≈ 2"):
- Systematic trial-and-error is a valid, full-mark approach
- A student who identifies the range, narrows it down, and finds a reasonable approximation → 3/3

Do NOT give 2/3 for not showing a formal algebraic derivation. Trial-and-error IS the expected method.

Henry's comment: *"非常棒!你是真正去嘗試慢慢增加"* (Excellent! You really tried increasing gradually)

---

## Products Equal Zero (equation-specific)

For equations of the form (expression₁)(expression₂) = 0:
- Each factor must be set to zero independently
- Missing even one factor → missing a solution → partial marks at most
- Henry's pattern: *"沒錯主要就是其中一個要是0"* (That's right, the key is that one must be zero)

Do NOT divide out a factor — factor instead, to keep all solutions visible.

---

## Reciprocal Substitution Insight (equation-specific)

When the same complex sub-expression appears repeatedly (e.g. `1/a` appears multiple times),
the cleanest path is to treat it as a single unknown (let u = 1/a).
Henry rewards students who find this shortcut: *"很棒! 那如果我把 1/a 整個當一個未知數呢"*
