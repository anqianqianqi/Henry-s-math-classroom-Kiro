Research note · Art direction

Two art directions, one website

The site is being repainted from the inside. A warm, hand-made storybook treatment has been landing since late July and now owns the most characteristic surfaces — but the bright, gamified layer it is replacing is still underneath almost everything, and the two meet on the same screens.

The two directions

What is being replaced, and what is replacing it

The older direction announces itself in the config: the Tailwind palette is commented Duolingo-inspired bright, friendly colors and the button shadow is the flat 4px press that style is known for. It is bright, cool, plastic, and high-contrast.

The newer one arrives across 1–2 August in a dense run of commits — a room that dissolves into the page instead of ending at a rectangle, a pencil frame drawn on the boundary, ink that samples the artwork it sits on, then painted-paper cards. It is warm, low contrast, and deliberately imprecise.

Findings

Where the two collide

Ordered by what a student actually sees. Each finding is tagged by the kind of problem it is: something already on screen, drift that is compounding quietly, or a decision that was declared and never took effect.

Ground On screen

Warm cards sit on a cool gradient

The painted card is a cream-to-ochre wash finished with brown pigment pooling. The default page ground underneath it runs mint green through white to translucent blue. Nothing is wrong with either; together the card reads as a stain on the page rather than a sheet of paper on a surface, because the ground is cooler than the thing it is supporting.

Card wash #fdf7e9 → #f4ecd6, pool rgba(176,138,88,.20)
Page ground from-primary-50 (#f0fdf4) via-white to-accent-blue/10
Reach 44 pages carry that gradient; Card is the default on 43 files
Type On screen

The site's two typefaces have never once loaded

Tailwind declares Inter for body and Poppins for display. No font is loaded anywhere — no next/font, no stylesheet link, no @font-face — and globals.css sets the body to Arial. The font-display and font-sans utilities are used zero times. Every word on the site is Arial or Helvetica, and has been the whole time.

This is the largest single gap between the intended identity and the shipped one, and the cheapest to close — but closing it changes the look of every page at once, so it is a decision, not a tidy-up.

Declared sans: Inter · display: Poppins
Loaded none — 0 matches for next/font, fonts.googleapis, @font-face
Applied font-display 0× · font-sans 0× · font-mono 7×
Actual body { font-family: Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif }
Edges On screen

“No rectangle survives” is true on two surfaces out of dozens

A week of work went into making artwork end as an unfinished drawing rather than a crop. It is used in two components. Meanwhile the same screens carry 52 hand-rolled containers with a hard white fill, a fixed radius and a drop shadow — each one the exact rectangle the treatment exists to remove. The idea is sound and under-distributed, not wrong.

The pet room is the honest test case: the soft edge was applied, broke, and was reverted, because its animated decorations render above the mask. That is an unsolved structural conflict, not a taste call.

Depth On screen

Buttons are pressed plastic; the paper they sit on has no shadow at all

The card deliberately gives up its drop shadow — a mask clips one outright — and separates from the page with inset pigment pooling instead. The button sitting inside that card uses a hard 4px offset shadow with no blur and drops it on press. Two opposite theories of depth, three centimetres apart.

Card inset 0 0 26px 6px rgba(176,138,88,.20), no border
Button shadow-float = 0 4px 0 0 rgb(0 0 0 / .1), active:shadow-none

Grounds On screen

Four page backgrounds, and feature areas are picking their own

Most of the site uses one gradient. Twelve pages use flat grey. The book-skins pages have quietly adopted a warm amber ground, and class discovery an indigo-to-purple one. The amber pair is interesting — it is the only place outside Card where the warm direction reached a page ground, and it got there independently.

Buttons On screen

“Secondary” is indistinguishable from a hovered primary

Primary is bg-primary-500 hovering to primary-600. Secondary is bg-primary-600. A secondary button therefore looks exactly like a primary button the pointer happens to be over. Where the two sit side by side — which is now every recipe panel, since Randomise and Invent are adjacent — there is no legible hierarchy between them.

primary bg-primary-500 hover:bg-primary-600
secondary bg-primary-600 hover:bg-primary-700
Chrome On screen

The installed app is blue; the website is green

themeColor in the root metadata is #2563eb. The brand primary is #22c55e. On Android and as an installed PWA the browser chrome and splash take the blue, so the first colour a student sees belongs to no part of either direction.

Generated On screen

The generated artwork has no house style, and just got 19,440 ways not to have one

Rooms and book bundles can be rendered in six mutually exclusive looks — photoreal, Ghibli, sci-fi, flat minimalist, antique lithograph, watercolour. Per asset that is a feature. In the shop grid, where a student sees a dozen at once, it means no two adjacent tiles necessarily belong to the same world.

Nothing constrains which styles may appear together in a student-facing collection; the constraints that exist all operate inside a single asset. This is the one finding created rather than inherited — the theme work of 1 August and 4 August widened the space without narrowing what may sit next to what.

ART_STYLES 6 render treatments, all reachable per asset
Cells 19,440 classification vectors, styleFor gates within one asset only
Unconstrained no rule spans a shop folder or a collection
Tokens Drift

Raw Tailwind colours outnumber the design tokens two to one

The palette in the config describes a smaller and smaller part of the site. Amber alone is used more than the entire primary ramp. That is not necessarily wrong — amber is warm, and warm is where the site is heading — but it means the direction is being set in class names rather than in the token layer, so nothing can be changed centrally.

Tokens primary.* 418 · accent.* 52  →  470
Off-token amber 419 · purple 186 · blue 176 · indigo 62 · +95  →  938
Radius Drift

Seven corner radii with no rule about which means what

Button is rounded-2xl, hand-rolled cards are mostly rounded-xl, inputs are rounded-lg, and the config bothers to redefine 2xl and 3xl. There is no stated relationship between radius and elevation, size, or role, so the value is chosen per component by whoever wrote it.

Card API Declared, inert

The escape hatch built for dense screens is used zero times

Card gained a plain prop for exactly the case where painted paper costs more than it gives — an admin list of forty rows. No page uses it. Those screens hand-roll a div instead, which means the intended two-tier system never came into being: in practice it is painted Card versus ad-hoc markup, with no shared vocabulary between them.

Dark Declared, inert

Dark mode is wired up and set to the light values

There is a prefers-color-scheme: dark block in globals.css, and it assigns exactly the same two variables as the light block. Someone deliberately neutralised it rather than deleting it. Worth deciding either way, because the painted-paper direction is a genuinely hard one to take dark — a cream wash has nowhere to go.

:root  → --background #ffffff, --foreground #171717
@media dark  → --background #ffffff, --foreground #171717

What this adds up to

The painted-paper direction is the stronger and more distinctive of the two, and it is the one being actively developed — but it currently exists as a treatment on a handful of components rather than as a system. It has no page ground, no typeface, no button, and no token in the config. Everything structural still belongs to the direction it is replacing.

That is why the collisions above are nearly all the same collision seen from different angles: a warm, soft, hand-made object placed inside a cool, hard, manufactured frame. Three decisions would resolve most of them at once.

  1. Give the warm direction a page ground and a token ramp, so cards stop being warm objects on a cool page.
  2. Decide the typeface question — ship Inter and Poppins, or delete them and choose deliberately. Today the site has no typographic identity at all.
  3. Decide whether the gamified button survives. It is the loudest surviving piece of the old direction and it appears on every screen.
